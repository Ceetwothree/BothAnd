## 1. Federal funding

### HUD Continuum of Care (CoC) Program
- HUD's FY2024 CoC Program Competition (awarded/announced January 17, 2025) gave the Los Angeles Continuum of Care (CA-600, the LAHSA-administered CoC covering LA City and most of LA County) **$220,238,668** for the 2025–2026 grant term. This included $215,482,074 to renew 130 projects (including $8,624,657 for five Youth Homelessness Demonstration Program projects), $3,256,594 for three new domestic-violence projects, and a $1,500,000 one-year planning grant. [LAHSA news, "FY 2024 & 2025 CoC Program NOFO"](https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=963-fy-2024-2025-coc-program-nofo)
- **2026 disruption**: On June 11, 2026, HUD suspended LAHSA from federal grant activity pending an Office of Inspector General investigation into alleged mismanagement — HUD OIG cited "repeated false statements" and failures around financial controls and conflict-of-interest safeguards. On June 18, 2026, HUD sent CA-600 a letter signaling intent to find that the LA CoC itself does not meet program requirements. LAHSA sued HUD, arguing the suspension violates the Administrative Procedure Act, separation of powers, and the Tenth Amendment. The suspension is reported to jeopardize **$241 million** in federal funding supporting more than 11,000 people county-wide, and the deadline for the LA region's next HUD CoC grant application was reported as August 26, 2026. [LAist, "LA's lead homelessness agency sues HUD"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/lahsa-homeless-los-angeles-sue-hud-trump-mismanagement-fraud-contiuum-of-care-funding); [NBC LA, "Federal funding for LAHSA suspended over 'failures'"](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/lahsa-homeless-funding-hud/3903074/)
- **Not verified**: I could not independently confirm the exact relationship/overlap between the $220.2M FY2024/25 CoC award figure and the $241M figure cited as "jeopardized" in 2026 reporting — these may refer to different grant years or include additional HUD funding streams beyond CoC. Flagging rather than reconciling.

### Emergency Solutions Grants (ESG)
- LA County received **$1,768,369** in federal ESG funds for FY2024–25 (Urban County allocation, administered by the LA County Development Authority (LACDA), which passes funds through to LAHSA). [California Grants Portal / LACDA notice](https://www.lacda.org/homelessness/emergency-solutions-grant)
- Additional ESG "RUSH" (disaster-response) funds were added via an amendment to the FY2024-25 Action Plan tied to the January 2025 wildfires (Federal Disaster Declaration DR-4856-CA). [LACDA public notice](https://www.lacda.org/public-notices/emergency-solutions-grants-rush)
- **Gap**: I could not find a separate, itemized City of LA ESG allocation (as distinct from the County/Urban County figure above) in this research pass.

## 3. County funding: Measure H → Measure A

### Measure H (2017–2025, now superseded)
- Measure H was a **quarter-cent** county sales tax ("Transactions and Use Tax to Prevent and Combat Homelessness"), effective March 2017, approved by LA County voters to fund homeless services (not housing construction — Measure H was explicitly a *services* tax; HHH was the City's separate housing *bond*). [LA County Auditor-Controller, Measure H Special Revenue Fund page](https://auditor.lacounty.gov/homeless-and-housing-measure-h-special-revenue-fund/)
- Measure H generated **about $500 million a year**, per LAist reporting. [LAist, "Measure A explained"](https://laist.com/measure-a-explained-keeping-up-with-la-countys-homelessness-initiative)
- Measure H had a legal sunset date of **2027** — it was not simply extended; it was repealed and replaced early by Measure A (below). [LAist, "Measure A explained"](https://laist.com/measure-a-explained-keeping-up-with-la-countys-homelessness-initiative)
- **Not verified**: A precise cumulative total of all Measure H revenue collected 2017–2025 (e.g., a "$3 billion+" lifetime total) was not found in a citable primary-source document in this research pass; do not report a cumulative figure without further sourcing.

### Measure A (2024 ballot measure, effective April 2025 — Measure H's replacement)
- LA County voters approved Measure A in **November 2024**. It repealed Measure H's quarter-cent tax and replaced it with a **half-cent** county sales tax, effective **April 1, 2025**. [NBC LA, "Measure A sales tax goes into effect"](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/measure-a-sales-tax-la-county-homeless/3667454/); [LA County Homeless Initiative, "Measure A"](https://homeless.lacounty.gov/measure-a/)
- Unlike Measure H, **Measure A has no sunset date** — it is permanent unless repealed by a future vote. [LAist, "Measure A explained"](https://laist.com/measure-a-explained-keeping-up-with-la-countys-homelessness-initiative)
- Projected/actual revenue: reported as **"over $1 billion annually"** in County Homeless Initiative materials, and as an **"estimated $1.1 billion annually"** in ballot-measure summaries. [LAist](https://laist.com/measure-a-explained-keeping-up-with-la-countys-homelessness-initiative); [National Housing Crisis Task Force case study](https://nationalhousingcrisis.org/case-study/los-angeles-ca-measure-a-in-2024-measure-hhh-in-2016/)
- Governance/oversight change: Measure A revenue is split among LA County, cities/Councils of Governments, the LA County Development Authority (LACDA), and the newly created **LA County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency (LACAHSA)**, which handles prevention and affordable-housing-construction funding. This is a structurally different oversight model than Measure H, which flowed more directly to LAHSA. [LAist](https://laist.com/measure-a-explained-keeping-up-with-la-countys-homelessness-initiative)
- **FY2026-27 Measure A Spending Plan**: The Board of Supervisors approved an **$843 million** Spending Plan for the new County Department of Homeless Services and Housing (HSH — see Section 5), funded by **$659.9 million** in anticipated Measure A tax revenue, **$62.8 million** in one-time Measure A carryover, **$48.4 million** in one-time Measure H carryover, and **$72.5 million** in state HHAP grant dollars. [LA County HSH FY26-27 Spending Plan board letter, Feb 3, 2026](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/hsh/1200764_BOSFILEDVERSION_FY2026-27MeasureASpendingPlan-FinalBoardLetter_2_1.pdf)
- Spending-plan program mix (FY2026-27) includes: **$277 million** for interim housing, **nearly $239 million** for permanent housing, 65 outreach teams (≈16,000 annual unsheltered engagements), 3,671 Time-Limited Subsidies, 3,675 locally-funded PSH rental subsidies, wraparound case management for 24,250 people in permanent housing, 6,185 interim housing beds, and a Homelessness Prevention Unit serving 750 households via predictive-risk targeting. [LA County HSH, FY26-27 Measure A Spending Plan PDF](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/hsh/1201032_FY2026-27MeasureASpendingPlan.pdf)

## 4. City funding: Measure HHH and Measure ULA

### Measure HHH (2016 bond)
- Voters approved a **$1.2 billion** general obligation bond in 2016 to build permanent supportive housing (and some interim/affordable housing) for people experiencing homelessness.
- **Spending to date**: As of **March 31, 2025**, a total of **$1.03 billion** had been spent across the PSH Loan and Facilities Programs, per a CAO status report. [CAO, HHH status report, cited via search of cao.lacity.gov/homeless/COC PDFs]
- **Units promised vs. delivered**: HHH was originally projected to produce roughly **10,000 units**; current tracking shows **8,091 total housing units** (6,578 of them supportive) across **125 projects** — meaning the program is expected to fall well short of its original 10,000-unit promise (one 2022 report specifically said only ~8,000 of the original 10,000 would ultimately be delivered). Of the 8,091: **14% were ready for occupancy, 54% in construction, 32% in pre-development** as of the most recent audit summarized in search results. There are also **103 projects** in the HHH Permanent Supportive pipeline and **14 projects** in the separate Innovative Housing Challenge pipeline. [ABC7, "Audit of LA's homelessness measure shows cost per housing unit as much as $837,000"](https://abc7.com/post/prop-hhh-housing-los-angeles-audit/11600642/); [LA City Controller, "The Problems and Progress of Prop. HHH"](https://controller.lacity.gov/audits/problems-and-progress-of-prop-hhh); [LA City Controller, "The High Cost of Homeless Housing: Review of Proposition HHH"](https://controller.lacity.gov/audits/high-cost-of-homeless-housing-hhh)
- **Cost overruns — this is the most heavily reported HHH figure**:
  - Original average per-unit cost was projected around **$350,000**.
  - By the 2022 Controller audit, median total development cost per unit had risen to **$580,155** (up 5.5% from the prior year's $549,608 median), with some individual projects reaching as high as **$837,000 per unit** (cited example: the 62-unit Vermont Manchester Senior Housing project on County-owned land, part of a larger mixed-use structure with retail, extra parking, and a community kitchen — meaning the $837K figure is a high-end outlier tied to a specific complex project, not a program-wide average).
  - Separately reported figures: average per-unit cost for projects in construction rose from **$531,000 (2020) to $596,846 (2021)**.
  - The maximum *HHH subsidy* (as distinct from total development cost) per unit was capped at **$140,000** in the most recent funding cycle, down from **$220,000** in earlier cycles — meaning HHH dollars cover a shrinking share of a rising total cost, with the rest made up by other funding stacks (tax credits, other loans).
  - [KTLA, "Los Angeles is spending up to $837,000 to house a single homeless person"](https://ktla.com/news/los-angeles-is-spending-up-to-837000-to-house-a-single-homeless-person/); [Reason, "Audit: L.A. Spending as Much as $837,000 per Unit"](https://reason.com/2022/02/25/audit-l-a-spending-as-much-as-837000-per-unit-of-housing-for-homeless/); [Courthouse News, "Audit: LA Homelessness Support Efforts Plagued by Delays, High Costs"](https://www.courthousenews.com/audit-la-homelessness-support-efforts-plagued-by-delays-high-costs/)
- Timeline: projects are taking **3 to 6 years** to complete, with the bulk originally projected to open between 2023 and 2026 — i.e., a decade after the bond was approved. [ABC7 audit summary](https://abc7.com/post/prop-hhh-housing-los-angeles-audit/11600642/)
- **Conflicting/unclear figures flagged**: Search-derived summaries did not clearly distinguish between the FY2022 Controller audit ("High Cost of Homeless Housing") and later annual updates ("The Problems and Progress of Prop. HHH") — these may be the same report referenced under two titles, or sequential annual audits; the Controller's office (under Ron Galperin, then Kenneth Mejia from Dec. 2022) is reported to have issued at least one HHH audit annually. A precise, current (2025/2026) median cost-per-unit figure, updated beyond the 2022 $580,155 figure, could not be confirmed in this pass — flagging as a gap.

### Measure ULA ("mansion tax," 2022)
- Voters approved Measure ULA in November 2022: an additional transfer tax on real property sales above certain thresholds within the City of LA (commonly summarized as 4% on sales $5M+ and 5.5% on sales $10M+, with thresholds indexed and periodically adjusted — e.g., an increase effective July 1 of a given year per AAGLA industry updates), funding affordable housing production, tenant assistance/eviction defense, and homelessness prevention. [AAGLA, "City of L.A. Measure ULA Update – Increased Thresholds Eff. July 1st"](https://members.aagla.org/news/city-of-la-measure-ula-update--increased-thresholds-eff-july-1st-)
- **Revenue — sources disagree / show a clear shortfall against original projections**:
  - Original city projections at passage: **$672 million to $1 billion annually**.
  - FY2022-23 (partial first year): **$15.6 million**.
  - FY2023-24: **$156.9 million** as of a January 2024 snapshot, with a year-end estimate of **$270.3 million** — reported as **55.3% below** the $604.6 million originally budgeted for that year.
  - FY2024-25 projection: **$271.1 million** (roughly flat, +0.3%, vs. the FY2023-24 estimate).
  - Cumulative: as of **January 2026**, the City reported Measure ULA had **surpassed $1 billion in total revenue collected since inception**. A separate report puts cumulative revenue at **$1.2 billion over three years** as of **May 2026**. Yet another figure — reported for the program's "first 26 months" — states ULA had brought in **just $725 million**, "barely 60% of the minimum estimated revenue" promised to voters.
  - **These three cumulative figures ($1B+ by Jan 2026; $1.2B by May 2026; $725M in "first 26 months") are not fully reconcilable from the sources found** — they may reflect different measurement windows (calendar vs. fiscal year), different cutoff dates, or different reporting methodologies (gross collections vs. net of refunds/administrative costs). Report all three, flagged, rather than picking one. [Hanson Bridgett, "The ULA Ordinance and Recent Updates"](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/publication/260211_7087_ula_ordinance_updates); [Matthews, "Changes to the Los Angeles Mansion Tax and Measure ULA"](https://www.matthews.com/insights/changes-to-los-angeles-mansion-tax-measure-ula); [Shelterforce, "What has Measure ULA Done so Far?" (Jan 2025)](https://shelterforce.org/2025/01/17/what-has-measure-ula-done-so-far/)
  - Underspending: as of a 2024 report, **a little more than $99 million** of collected ULA funds remained unspent. [Shelterforce, "What has Measure ULA Done so Far?"](https://shelterforce.org/2025/01/17/what-has-measure-ula-done-so-far/)
- **Litigation**: The Howard Jarvis Taxpayers Association (HJTA) and the Apartment Association of Greater Los Angeles (AAGLA) sued jointly to invalidate Measure ULA, arguing it violates Article XIII A of the California Constitution (the Prop 13 framework governing special taxes). As of reporting found, the appeal was fully briefed and pending oral argument before California's **Fourth Appellate District Court of Appeal**. [Hanson Bridgett](https://www.hansonbridgett.com/publication/260211_7087_ula_ordinance_updates); [HJTA, "The Legal Front: Update on the Challenge to Measure ULA" (Spring 2026)](https://www.hjta.org/news-events/taxing-times/taxing-times-online-spring-2026-vol-52-issue-2/the-legal-front-update-on-the-challenge-to-measure-ula-the-mansion-tax/)
- **Not verified**: I could not confirm a final appellate ruling in this research pass — as of the most recent source found, the litigation was still pending oral argument, meaning ULA's ultimate legal status was unresolved as of this writing.

## 5. Combined city/county totals and recent budget stress

- Commonly cited aggregate: **"The City and County of Los Angeles spend about $2 billion a year tackling homelessness"** (a rough combined figure from search-derived reporting; treat as an approximation, not an audited combined total, since city and county budgets use different fiscal years and accounting methods). One source separately breaks this down as roughly "**nearly $1 billion**" annually from the City and a "**$637 million** annual homeless budget" from the County — these two figures alone sum to about $1.6B, not $2B, illustrating how loosely these aggregate claims are constructed across sources. A third figure cites LA city and county **"expected to spend nearly $3 billion a year"** — again, not reconciled with the other two. **All three aggregate figures are flagged as inconsistent; none should be treated as authoritative without checking the underlying city/county adopted budgets directly.**
- FY2025-26 City of LA budget: reported **$900 million** overall for homelessness. FY2025-26 LA County Board of Supervisors approved **$908 million** in funding recommendations for homeless services.
- FY2023-24 LA County Homeless Initiative budget: **$609.7 million**, described at the time as the largest annual county investment to date. FY2024-25 approved County spending plan: **$783 million** proposed, with **$662.3 million** allocated to "essential elements of the homeless services system."
- **Underspending / budget-cycle irregularities**:
  - A December 2024 report found the City of LA left **$473 million** (one source) / **$513 million** (another source) unspent from its homeless services budget in FY2025 — sources differ on the exact figure ($473M vs. $513M); both describe substantial year-over-year underspending rather than a funding shortage.
  - Separately, the City cut nearly **$200 million** in homeless funding amid a broader city budget deficit (reported by Washington Examiner, so treat as a single non-primary source pending corroboration).
  - [LAist, "LA sat on nearly half a billion dollars in homelessness funds last year"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/la-sat-on-nearly-half-a-billion-dollars-in-homelessness-funds-last-year); [Fox LA, "LA to track homeless budget after $513M went unspent"](https://www.foxla.com/news/los-angeles-homeless-spending); [Washington Examiner, "LA cuts nearly $200 million in homeless funds amid budget crunch"](https://www.washingtonexaminer.com/policy/finance/4445715/los-angeles-cut-200-million-homeless-service-deficit/)

### LA County's institutional break from LAHSA (2025-2026)
This is a major structural change to "where the money goes" that any funding narrative needs to account for:
- On **April 1, 2025**, the LA County Board of Supervisors voted **4–0** (one abstention, Supervisor Holly Mitchell) to create a new county department to take over homeless services functions previously contracted out to LAHSA.
- The Board approved the name **LA County Department of Homeless Services and Housing (HSH)** on July 1, 2025; the department officially launched **January 1, 2026**.
- The County approved **over $840 million** for this new department's initial operations.
- This is distinct from **LACAHSA** (LA County Affordable Housing Solutions Agency, ~$385 million cited in one source), which separately handles affordable-housing-production and prevention funding, and from **LACDA** (LA County Development Authority, ~$32.3 million cited), which continues to administer certain federal pass-through funds (e.g., ESG).
- Reasons cited: years of criticism that LAHSA was slow to act and lacked accountability, echoing a November 2023 County Auditor-Controller audit (see Section 6).
- [LAist, "LA County launches new homelessness department"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/los-angeles-county-homeless-services-and-housing-department-launch-lahsa); [NBC LA, "LA County approves over $840M for its own homelessness agency"](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/news/local/los-angeles-county-department-of-homeless-services-and-housing-funding/3840891/); [Supervisor Lindsey Horvath, "LA County Takes Action for a More Accountable Homeless Services System"](https://lindseyhorvath.lacounty.gov/new-homeless-department/)

### LAHSA's own budget, shrinking as County funding exits
- LAHSA's total approved budget for FY2024-25 was reported at **approximately $914.8 million**, with **$302.7 million** from the City general fund and the remaining **$601.4 million** from "special funds" (county, state, federal, and other sources).
- Historical FY2022-23 breakdown: County contributed roughly **$246 million**, City roughly **$234 million** (with the remainder from state/federal sources).
- As the County pulled its funding to stand up its own department (above), the **LAHSA Commission approved a $496 million budget for FY2026-27** — a **40% decrease** from the **$829 million** approved the prior year. LAHSA's remaining budget is now reported as **73% funded by the City of LA** alone, reflecting its narrowing role now that the County has exited.

## 6. Audits: waste, delay, and oversight gaps

### 2024 California State Auditor report (the flagship state-level accountability audit)
- **Report**: "Homelessness in California: The State Must Do More to Assess the Effectiveness of Homelessness Programs," California State Auditor, Report **2023-102.1**, released **April 9, 2024**. [California State Auditor, Report 2023-102.1](https://information.auditor.ca.gov/reports/2023-102.1/index.html); [PDF](https://www.auditor.ca.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/2023-102.1-Report.pdf)
- **Headline finding**: California spent **$24 billion** on homelessness efforts across **fiscal years 2018 through 2023** (roughly five years) but has not consistently tracked the spending or the outcomes of the programs it funded. Lead auditor Grant Parks stated the state "has not collected sufficient data" to demonstrate the money improved the situation.
- **Program-level finding**: The audit evaluated five state homelessness programs; only **two** were found "likely" to be cost-effective. One was **Homekey** (Governor Newsom's signature hotel/motel-to-housing conversion program), found to have an average cost of **$144,000 per unit** — notably far below the HHH per-unit costs cited in Section 4, though Homekey and HHH are structurally different programs (acquisition/conversion vs. new construction).
- **City-level accountability gaps**: San José and San Diego were both cited as having identified hundreds of millions of dollars in homelessness-related spending but, absent a formal spending plan or tracking mechanism, neither could definitively identify all of its relevant revenues and expenditures — evidence the tracking problem is not unique to LA.
- **Recommendations**: The audit recommended the state's California Interagency Council on Homelessness (**Cal ICH**) require state agencies administering homelessness programs to report spending and outcome data, and develop and publish a public accountability "scorecard" on homelessness programs by **March 2025**.
- [Breitbart, "Audit: California Lost Track of $24 Billion Spent to Combat Homelessness"](https://www.breitbart.com/politics/2024/04/12/audit-california-lost-track-24-billion-spent-combat-homelessness/amp/); [Governing, "California Doesn't Know If the Billions Spent on Homelessness Helped"](https://www.governing.com/management-and-administration/california-doesnt-know-if-the-billions-spent-on-homelessness-helped); [CalMatters, "Audit: California fails to track its homelessness spending, outcomes"](https://calmatters.org/housing/homelessness/2024/04/california-homelessness-spending/); [CBS SF, coverage of the audit](https://www.cbsnews.com/sanfrancisco/news/california-homelessness-spending-audit-24b-five-years-didnt-consistently-track-outcomes/)
- This $24 billion / 5-year figure is the number later invoked by U.S. Attorney Bill Essayli (below) in announcing a federal fraud task force — it has become the anchor statistic for most subsequent "money wasted" narratives about California/LA homelessness spending. It refers to **statewide** spending, not LA-specific spending — a distinction worth preserving in any writeup that uses it.

### 2022–2023 LA independent/County audit into $600M–$2.4B in tracked spending
- In 2022, **U.S. District Judge David O. Carter** (presiding over the LA Alliance for Human Rights v. City/County of LA litigation, filed 2020) publicly raised concerns about the city's inability to track **$600 million** in homelessness funding, and later ordered an independent audit.
- That court-ordered independent audit examined **about $2.4 billion** the City of LA spent over a **four-year period** (reported as ending in the prior year relative to the audit's release). It found the City had made it "impossible to accurately track homelessness spending," largely because of outsourcing to LAHSA, which had "failed to collect accurate data on its vendors and hold them accountable." Judge Carter warned he might appoint an outside receiver to take over the City's homelessness funds absent improved transparency.
- A related/updated figure from later reporting describes a **2025** court-ordered audit finding LA did not properly track **$2.3 billion** in homeless-program spending, with information gaps and incomplete documentation hindering outcome measurement. **This $2.3B (2025) and $2.4B (city, four-year) figures are close but not identical — likely reflect different reporting periods or slightly different scopes; not fully reconciled here.**
- [LAist, "Searing audit finds city of LA has failed to properly track billions in homelessness spending"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/audit-homeless-carter-lahsa); [KTLA, "Scathing audit finds L.A. homeless spending lacks oversight, accountability"](https://ktla.com/news/local-news/los-angeles-homeless-spending-audit/); [Westside Current, "LAHSA's 18-Year Record of Audit Failures on Homeless Spending Laid Bare in Federal Court"](https://www.westsidecurrent.com/news/lahsa-s-18-year-record-of-audit-failures-on-homeless-spending-laid-bare-in-federal/article_5c30a8e1-2e35-4062-8d43-2c3dd5fb2b47.html)

### LA County Auditor-Controller review of LAHSA (finance/contracts/risk/grants), released November 2024
- Formal title referenced in filings: "LAHSA Finance, Contracts, Risk Management, and Grants Management Review," presented to the Board of Supervisors around **February 27, 2024** and covered again in coverage dated November 19, 2024 — dates in search snippets are inconsistent; treat the November 2024 date as the widely reported release/coverage date. [LA County Auditor-Controller PDF](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/auditor/cmr/1170598_2024-11-19LAHSA-Finance_Contracts_RiskManagement_andGrantsManagementReview_February27_2024_BoardAgendaItem4_.pdf)
- **Cash advances not recovered**: LAHSA gave service providers cash advances totaling nearly **$51 million** starting in 2017 (using County taxpayer dollars) without establishing formal repayment agreements. By July 2024, only **$2.5 million** had been repaid. Auditors separately identified an additional **$15 million** in outstanding advances to other providers, some dating to 2016, also unpaid. (Note: an earlier/different reporting round cited "$50.8 million in provider advances, only $220,000 had been recovered" — these figures are in the same ballpark but not identical to the $51M/$2.5M figures above; both may be accurate at different snapshot dates as more repayments trickled in.)
- **Late payments**: In a sample of payments between July 2023 and May 2024, LAHSA paid 5 of 13 sampled subcontractors late; auditors found at least one instance where a County subcontractor was paid using funds from an unrelated funding source 14 days before LAHSA had even received the County funding meant to cover that cost.
- **Weak contract monitoring**: About **51%** of LAHSA's planned contract compliance reviews did not include methods for verifying providers' compliance with service-delivery requirements.
- Overall auditor assessment: "a lack of adequate internal controls at LAHSA related to finances, contracts, risk management and grants management."
- This audit is widely cited as the direct trigger for the County's 2025 decision to build its own Department of Homeless Services and Housing rather than continue routing County homelessness dollars through LAHSA (see Section 5).
- [Westside Current, "Audit Reveals LAHSA's Financial Mismanagement: Over $50M in Taxpayer Funds Unaccounted For"](https://www.westsidecurrent.com/news/audit-reveals-lahsa-s-financial-mismanagement-over-50m-in-taxpayer-funds-unaccounted-for/article_0c69ab7c-ab8e-11ef-853a-ffdb2db76109.html); [LAist, "Following scathing audit, LA County supervisor proposes moving homeless services under direct county control"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/los-angeles-homeless-services-authority-lahsa-audit-2024-november-county)

### City Controller audit: empty shelter beds, December 2024
- Released by **City Controller Kenneth Mejia**, examining City interim-housing programs from **2019 to 2023**.
- Finding: roughly **a quarter of shelter beds sat empty each night** across the period, at a cumulative cost of **$218 million** in wasted capacity from 2019–2023 (about **$68 million per year** at current, larger shelter capacity).
- Occupancy target was 95%; actual occupancy **did not exceed 78% in any year** studied.
- Outcomes: **fewer than a fifth** of people placed in interim housing went on to secure permanent housing — a rate the audit itself called "woefully inadequate."
- Paradox noted in the audit: despite the high vacancy rate, **nearly a third** of people who expressed interest in a shelter bed were not able to secure one — pointing to a matching/placement-process failure rather than a true capacity shortfall.
- [The Center Square, "Los Angeles audit finds quarter of shelter beds empty each night: $218MM loss"](https://www.thecentersquare.com/california/article_eff5afcc-b817-11ef-bd1c-f395ced5f0a0.html); [KTLA, "Audit finds 1 in 4 shelter beds in Los Angeles went unused, costing taxpayers $218 million"](https://ktla.com/news/local-news/audit-finds-1-in-4-shelter-beds-in-los-angeles-went-unused-costing-taxpayers-218-million/)

## 8. Outcomes context: does the spending correlate with fewer people homeless?

- LAHSA's finalized **2025 Greater LA Homeless Count** (released after HUD review) found homelessness in LA County fell **4%** to **72,308** people, and in the City of LA fell **3.4%** to **43,699** — the **second consecutive annual decline**. [LAHSA, "LAHSA Releases Finalized 2025 Homeless Count Results After HUD Review"](https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=1051-lahsa-releases-finalized-2025-homeless-count-results-after-hud-review); [LAist, "Homelessness in LA region dropped for the second time in two years"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/homelessness-la-region-annual-count-2025)
- **Unsheltered** homelessness dropped more sharply: down **9.5%** countywide and **7.9%** in the City in the 2025 count alone; over the two-year period, unsheltered homelessness is reported down **14%** countywide and **17.5%** in the City.
- LAHSA attributed the decline partly to encampment clearing and to nearly **28,000 people placed into permanent housing** in the prior year — a reported record high — plus an **8.5% increase** in people entering interim housing (shelters).
- **Contested counterpoint**: A RAND report ("Growing Inaccuracies in Official Counts Jeopardize LA Homelessness Wins") raises methodological concerns about the reliability of the official Point-in-Time Count itself — worth noting as a caveat on any "spending caused this decline" claim. [RAND, "Growing Inaccuracies in Official Counts Jeopardize LA Homelessness Wins"](https://www.rand.org/pubs/research_reports/RRA4438-1.html)
- **Causation caveat (explicit)**: None of the sources reviewed directly establish a causal link between specific dollar amounts spent (HHAP rounds, Measure A/H revenue, HHH bond spending) and the count's decline. The count decline coincides with a period of record encampment-clearing activity, increased interim shelter capacity, and record permanent-housing placements — but also with well-documented underspending ($473M-$513M left unspent in FY2025 alone, per Section 5) and the HUD funding suspension/fraud investigations described in Section 1 and 6. A responsible presentation of this material should state the trend (declining count) and the spending problems (tracking failures, waste, fraud investigations) as parallel, not resolve them into a single "spending worked" or "spending failed" narrative — the sources found do not support either strong claim.

## 9. Philanthropic / private funding

- Private philanthropy's role appears **meaningful but small relative to public dollars** — no source found quantifies it as a percentage of total LA homelessness spending (which runs into the hundreds of millions to ~$2 billion+ annually from public sources per Section 5).
- **Conrad N. Hilton Foundation** is repeatedly described as the largest philanthropic funder of homelessness solutions in LA. Cumulative figures found: **over $80 million in grants** related to ending chronic homelessness over roughly two decades, of which **$56 million** was LA-focused specifically. Individual grant examples: **$7.8 million** for the "Home For Good" public-private-philanthropic action plan (targeting chronic/veteran homelessness by 2016); an **$18 million** public-private partnership fund for housing high-need homeless patients in LA County; **$5 million over two years** to the Corporation for Supportive Housing; **$1.525 million each** to the Downtown Women's Center and The People Concern (recent grants). [Hilton Foundation news releases](https://www.hiltonfoundation.org/news/hilton-foundation-announces-7-8-million-grant-to-end-chronic-homelessness-in-los-angeles-county/); [Hilton Foundation, $18M public-private fund](https://www.hiltonfoundation.org/news/public-private-partnership-creates-18-million-fund-to-provide-housing-for-high-need-homeless-patients-in-los-angeles-county/)
- Broader context (statewide, not LA-specific): one opinion/analysis piece states **California spends less than 0.5% of its state-controlled funds on homelessness** — included here as framing context on the scale of public spending relative to the state budget, not as a claim about philanthropy. [The Conversation, "California spends less than 0.5% of its state-controlled funds on homelessness"](https://theconversation.com/california-spends-less-than-0-5-of-its-state-controlled-funds-on-homelessness-284422) — **this is an opinion/analysis source, not an audited figure; flagged accordingly.**
- **Gap**: No source found gives a comprehensive dollar total for *all* private/philanthropic giving toward LA homelessness in a given year (e.g., an aggregated figure across all foundations, corporate giving, and individual donations) comparable to the public-funding totals above. This would require a dedicated philanthropic-sector data source (e.g., Candid/Foundation Directory, a regional philanthropy association report) not accessed in this research pass.
