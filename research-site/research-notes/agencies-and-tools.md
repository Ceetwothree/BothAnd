# Agencies and Tools: The Institutional and Technical Architecture of LA's Homelessness Response

*Research notes compiled August 1, 2026. This document maps the government agencies, quasi-government bodies, nonprofit service providers, and data/technology systems involved in Los Angeles City and County's homelessness response, with sourced claims and explicit notes on gaps and disputes. Because this sector is in the middle of a historic institutional rupture (LAHSA's county funding withdrawal, a new county department, and a federal funding suspension all landed within the same 12 months), dates matter enormously — figures and structures described here can be superseded quickly.*

---

## Note on timing: this is a moving target

As of this writing (August 2026), Los Angeles is mid-collapse-and-rebuild of its core homelessness governance structure. In brief, so the rest of this document makes sense:

- **April 2025**: LA County Board of Supervisors voted 4-0 to move funding and staff out of LAHSA and stand up a new county department. ([California County News, April 2025](https://www.californiacountynews.org/news/2025/04/la-county-strip-millions-lahsa-create-its-own-agency); [CalMatters, May 2025](https://calmatters.org/housing/homelessness/2025/05/la-homeless-agency/))
- **January 1, 2026**: The new **LA County Department of Homeless Services and Housing (HSH)** officially launched, absorbing the county's Housing for Health division (formerly under the Department of Health Services) and the Homeless Initiative. ([LAist, via search summary](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/la-county-new-homeless-department-housing-for-health-unhoused); [Northridge East Neighborhood Council, Jan 2026](https://nenc-la.org/2026/01/welcome-to-the-new-la-county-department-of-homeless-services-and-housing-hsh/))
- **February 3, 2026**: County Board of Supervisors approved an **$843 million FY2026-27 spending plan** for the new HSH department. ([lacounty.gov press release, Feb 3, 2026](https://lacounty.gov/2026/02/03/la-county-aims-to-keep-people-sheltered-and-housed-by-focusing-on-proven-programs/))
- **By July 1, 2026**: Roughly $300 million in county Measure A funding was slated to move fully out of LAHSA and into HSH. ([LAist](https://laist.com/brief/news/housing-homelessness/lahsa-homeless-exit-vote-horvath-barger); [CBS LA](https://www.cbsnews.com/losangeles/news/la-county-supervisors-votes-to-create-massive-new-homelessness-department))
- **June 11-12, 2026**: HUD suspended federal funding to LAHSA (and, by extension, jeopardized the LA Continuum of Care's federal grant pipeline), citing fraud allegations and financial-controls failures. ([HUD press release](https://www.hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-048); [FOX 11](https://www.foxla.com/news/hud-suspends-los-angeles-lahsa-homeless-funding-fraud))
- **June 29, 2026**: LAHSA sued HUD over the suspension.
- **July 2, 2026**: A federal judge (David O. Carter) granted a stay, ordering funding to continue pending litigation, with a hearing set for August 6, 2026. ([Beverly Press, July 2026](https://beverlypress.com/2026/07/judge-rules-federal-funding-to-lahsa-must-continue/); [LAist](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/judge-wants-to-preserve-current-federal-funding-for-las-lead-homelessness-agency-amid-legal-battle))
- **LAHSA's FY2026-27 budget** was approved at **$496 million**, a roughly 40% drop from the prior year's ~$829 million, reflecting the county's withdrawal and reduced state dollars; LAHSA is now about 73% funded by the City of LA alone. ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/lahsa-approves-budget-homeless-la))

**Gap flag:** As of August 1, 2026, it is not resolved whether LAHSA will continue to exist as a joint powers authority in a reduced (city-only) form, be dissolved, or be restructured again — nor is it resolved whether the LA City Council will follow through on its own vote to explore withdrawing its funding. Anything written about LAHSA's future should be checked against reporting dated after August 6, 2026 (the next federal court hearing).

### LAHSA — structure and the accountability problem

LAHSA is a **joint powers authority (JPA)**, created in 1993 specifically so LA City and LA County would stop fighting over which government "owned" homelessness. It is governed by a **10-member Commission**: 5 members appointed by the County Board of Supervisors, 5 by the Mayor with City Council confirmation. ([LAist explainer, summarized via search](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/la-los-angeles-homeless-homelessness-services-authority-lahsa); [Law Insider LAHSA definition](https://www.lawinsider.com/dictionary/lahsa))

The JPA structure is the crux of LA's accountability problem: **neither government fully owns LAHSA, and both have equal veto-like power over it**, which is precisely why the 2025-2026 rupture became so messy — the county could vote to defund LAHSA, but the JPA agreement still nominally gives it equal governing power, and the city cannot unilaterally restructure or abolish it either. This is a structural design flaw noted repeatedly in coverage of the breakup. ([California County News](https://www.californiacountynews.org/news/2025/04/la-county-strip-millions-lahsa-create-its-own-agency))

LAHSA also serves as the **Collaborative Applicant** for the HUD-recognized **LA Continuum of Care (CoC)** — meaning LAHSA is not just "a" homeless-services agency but the entity that legally applies for and administers nearly $1 billion in cumulative federal CoC funding on behalf of the entire county's homelessness system. This dual role (regional operator + CoC administrator) is why HUD's June 2026 funding suspension threatens the entire county's system, not just LAHSA's own programs — LAHSA's CoC role means a huge share of provider contracts flow through it. ([LAHSA CoC page, summarized via search](https://www.lahsa.org/coc/); [CalMatters, June 2026](https://calmatters.org/housing/homelessness/2026/06/lahsa-hud-funding/))

**Leadership churn**: LAHSA has had unstable leadership through this period. CEO Va Lecia Adams Kellum resigned after revelations that $2.1 million in federal funds LAHSA oversaw went to a nonprofit that employed her husband — a conflict-of-interest finding cited directly in HUD's suspension letter. Gita O'Neill (formerly the LA City Attorney's first director of homeless strategies) was appointed interim CEO by a 7-1 Commission vote effective August 26, 2025. ([search summary of LAHSA appointment announcement](https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=1046-lahsa-commission-appoints-gita-o-neill-as-interim-ceo); [HotAir/Legal Insurrection summary of HUD letter](https://legalinsurrection.com/2026/06/hud-suspends-federal-homeless-funding-to-los-angeles-in-wake-of-fraud-allegations/))

**The 2022-2024 audit trail**: A LA County Auditor-Controller review (finalized ~February 2024, building on findings reported through 2022-2023) found LAHSA:
- Could not produce an accurate list of the agencies it contracts with, and made payments with no contract in place ("doesn't know who it's paying, what it's paying for, nor how much it's paying").
- Had recovered only $2.5 million of $50.8 million in Measure H funds advanced in FY2017-18, with $8 million still outstanding from advances made 2016-2023.
- Failed to complete any of four planned internal audits in FY2022-23, and started only two of four planned in FY2023-24.
- Had "almost no meaningful performance measures" in service contracts, and did not enforce the few it had.
([LA County Auditor-Controller report, Feb 2024, PDF](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/auditor/cmr/1170598_2024-11-19LAHSA-Finance_Contracts_RiskManagement_andGrantsManagementReview_February27_2024_BoardAgendaItem4_.pdf); [Westside Current summary](https://www.westsidecurrent.com/news/calls-to-defund-lahsa-grow-after-audit-reveals-widespread-financial-failures/article_7f5a9804-fafc-11ef-8994-578edda82ead.html))

A separate 2026 U.S. District Court-ordered audit process (Judge Carter has long overseen LA homelessness litigation, e.g., the *LA Alliance for Human Rights* case) and reporting found the city had "failed to properly track billions in homelessness spending." ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/audit-homeless-carter-lahsa))

**HUD's June 2026 suspension** cited a "clear pattern of fraud," failure to track empty hotel/motel rooms being billed to programs, a 2024 audit showing **$513 million in unspent funds**, and the $2.1 million conflict-of-interest contract. The suspension put an estimated $150 million in already-awarded-but-not-finalized grants in limbo and blocked LAHSA from submitting the region's next ~$241 million CoC funding application. ([search summary of HUD/legalinsurrection/CalMatters reporting](https://calmatters.org/housing/homelessness/2026/06/lahsa-hud-funding/))

**Gap/caution flag:** This document could not directly access the original HUD press release (hud.gov/news/hud-no-26-048) or LAHSA's own site due to a tooling/network restriction during research; the figures above are drawn from multiple independent news outlets' summaries of that release and should be cross-checked against the primary HUD document when accessible.

### LA Continuum of Care (CoC)

The CoC is the HUD-mandated regional planning structure required for any community receiving HUD homeless-assistance funding — it is not synonymous with LAHSA but is administered through it. LAHSA is the CoC's "Collaborative Applicant," and the CoC has its own board (distinct from, but overlapping in practice with, the LAHSA Commission). ([LAHSA CoC Board page reference found in search, not independently verified due to fetch access issues](https://www.lahsa.org/coc/board)) This overlap — one staff, one lead agency, arguably two governing bodies with related but not identical membership — is itself a source of the accountability confusion critics have pointed to, since it can be unclear whether a given failure is a "LAHSA" failure or a "CoC" failure structurally, even though in practice they are the same operation.

### LA County departments

- **DHS / Housing for Health**: Founded 2012 within DHS to serve homeless patients cycling through county hospitals; ran an integrated continuum from street outreach to permanent housing with case management and clinical services. Reported a 90% prevention-success rate and 94% one-year housing retention rate for PSH clients in its last year under DHS. Moved to the new HSH department Jan 1, 2026, and its former budget/staffing became foundational to HSH's design — county officials explicitly describe Housing for Health as "the model for what's next." ([LAist, summarized via search](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/la-county-new-homeless-department-housing-for-health-unhoused))
- **DMH**: Runs the HOME (Homeless Outreach and Mobile Engagement) program — field-based mental-health outreach teams, including psychiatrists doing literal sidewalk assessments and even sidewalk court hearings for people too unwell/averse to come to a clinic. Present in all 8 county Service Planning Areas. ([NAMI Greater LA County](https://namiglac.org/los-angeles-county-department-of-mental-health-homeless-outreach-mobile-engagement-home-program/); [DMH HOME page](https://dmh.lacounty.gov/our-services/countywide-services/home/))
- **DPH**: Runs mobile medical clinics into encampments, infectious-disease response, and (via its Substance Abuse Prevention and Control division) counselors doing encampment/shelter-based outreach for substance-use treatment; also issues county-wide Encampment Resolution Guidance for local jurisdictions. ([LA County Encampment Resolution Guidance PDF](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1183858_LACountyEncampmentResolutionGuidance.pdf))
- **DPSS**: A structurally distinct track — administers **CalWORKs Homeless Assistance** (temporary daily shelter payments, permanent HA for move-in/back-rent, a county-added 14-day THAP+14 supplement, rental/moving assistance) but only for cash-aid-eligible families with children. This is welfare-system homelessness assistance, separate from and not coordinated through the CES/HMIS system that governs the broader adult homeless-services system — a potential structural gap for families who don't qualify for CalWORKs. ([DPSS CalWORKs Homeless page](https://dpss.lacounty.gov/en/cash/calworks/homeless.html))

### LA City departments and offices

- **LAHD**: Finances affordable/supportive housing development (using the ULA "mansion tax," 70% of which funds affordable housing production and 30% funds homelessness prevention) and administers Homekey (motel/hotel-to-housing conversions). In December 2025, the city created a new **Homelessness Oversight Bureau (HOB)** within LAHD — explicitly built to be "a central entity for decision-making, coordinating, managing, and overseeing the City's homelessness system," combining 11 existing LAHD positions with 9 positions moved over from the CAO. ([search summary of city budget/LAHD documents](https://cityclerk.lacity.org/onlinedocs/2025/25-0207-s1_rpt_lahd_10-30-25.pdf))
- **Mayor's Office / Inside Safe**: Not a department but a mayoral initiative launched by Mayor Karen Bass in December 2022. Operates through a "Field Intervention Team (FIT)" — described as including people with lived experience of homelessness, incarceration, and addiction recovery — that works encampment-by-encampment, offering hotel/motel rooms in exchange for voluntary encampment clearance, then partners with outreach providers (e.g., USC Street Medicine) for medical/social services. More than 21,000 people are reported to have "come inside" through the program cumulatively by 2026 reporting, though **transparency has reportedly declined**: the city stopped releasing detailed person-by-person outcome numbers it published in the program's first year, and some past operations are listed simply as "pending" with no update — a gap Councilmember Monica Rodriguez has said she's raised repeatedly without resolution. City Controller Kenneth Mejia announced an audit of Inside Safe after a federal judge said the city had misled attorneys about its progress on clearing encampments and adding shelter beds. Other reporting pegs cumulative Inside Safe costs above $322 million with comparatively modest permanent-housing placement numbers (one investigation cited $67 million spent for 255 people permanently housed at one point in the program). ([Mayor's office Inside Safe page](https://mayor.lacity.gov/InsideSafe); [Westside Current cost figures](https://www.westsidecurrent.com/news/bass-to-end-homeless-emergency-as-inside-safe-costs-top-322-million-with-meager-results/article_33f507f8-399f-451e-867b-8ca003852939.html); [NBC LA investigation](https://www.nbclosangeles.com/investigations/la-mayor-inside-safe-homeless-housing-program/3281201/); [KCRW on operational issues](https://www.kcrw.com/news/shows/greater-la/karen-bass-everything-everywhere-oc/inside-safe))
- **CAO Homelessness Initiatives**: The City Administrative Officer's office produces the recurring "Comprehensive Homeless Strategy" reports (jointly with the Chief Legislative Analyst) and handles cross-departmental budget/funding-source coordination (HHAP, Measure A Local Solutions Fund, general fund) for city homelessness spending, including managing Inside Safe's quarterly funding advances to LAHSA. ([CAO homeless strategy page](https://cao.lacity.gov/homeless/index.htm); [CAO reports on Council file system](https://cityclerk.lacity.org/onlinedocs/2023/23-1022-s33_rpt_cao_5-12-26.pdf))
- **City Council field deployment**: Each of the 15 council districts maintains its own "Homelessness Deputy"/"Homelessness Liaison" staff who independently coordinate outreach, sanitation, and encampment response within that district, working under a "Homelessness Coordinator" and "Homelessness Liaison Team Lead" model, and separately coordinating with mutual-aid groups and contracted crews (e.g., LA Conservation Corps) for spot cleanups. This means encampment response is **partly balkanized by council district** — a resident's experience of city response can depend heavily on which of 15 offices covers their block, and coordination between district approaches (e.g., one council office pursuing a more sweep-heavy posture vs. another prioritizing service-first engagement) is a documented point of friction; one 2025 profile noted a councilmember who had publicly called sweeps "inhumane" shifting her approach once in office and facing the same operational pressures. ([LA Public Press profile](https://lapublicpress.org/2025/11/hernandez-encampment-sweeps/); [example district homelessness pages](https://cd4.lacity.gov/initiative/homelessness/), [cd13.lacity.gov](https://cd13.lacity.gov/issues/homelessness))

### Housing authorities: HACLA and LACDA

- **HACLA** (city) administers Section 8 and public housing for over 83,000 households citywide, and is a critical — if strained — piece of the exit ramp from homelessness: LAHSA frequently refers unhoused clients to HACLA for voucher assistance, and HACLA runs 219 permanent-supportive-housing project-based-voucher developments (10,322 units) plus HUD-VASH vouchers for homeless veterans (5,115 total city allocation as of 2024). But HACLA **paused processing new Section 8 applications for 3,300+ families starting March 2025** amid a ~$118 million federal funding shortfall tied to federal budget uncertainty, illustrating how a voucher-supply chokepoint upstream (federal appropriations) can undercut the "exit" side of the local homelessness system even when outreach/shelter capacity exists. ([LAist](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/hacla-unhoused-la-homelessness); [KTLA](https://ktla.com/news/local-news/l-a-housing-authority-pauses-section-8-housing-applications-amid-federal-budget-uncertainty/); [HACLA notice](https://www.hacla.org/en/news/hacla-notifies-section-8-participants-changes-program-due-recent-budget-cuts))
- **LACDA** (county) is the equivalent for unincorporated county areas plus 62 contract cities — administers Section 8, owns ~3,000 public housing units at 63 sites, and runs a Homeless Incentive Program that pays landlords to accept homeless-preference Section 8 holders, plus VASH vouchers coordinated with the VA Medical Center of Greater LA. ([LACDA overview, summarized via search](https://www.lacda.org/))
- **LACAHSA** is a newer, distinct entity (created 2023-2025) that is *not* a housing authority in the traditional voucher-administering sense but a regional affordable-housing production/preservation and tenant-protection agency that receives over a third of Measure A revenue — it matters to homelessness because it is meant to grow the supply of affordable units that the CoC/HSH system depends on for exits, and because its renter-protection/eviction-prevention spending ($114M+ in FY2026-27) is meant to reduce inflow into homelessness. ([LA Forward Institute explainer](https://www.laforward.institute/report-forward/what-is-lacahsa))

### Where mandates overlap and where people fall through gaps

1. **LAHSA vs. the new county HSH department vs. the CoC**: As of mid-2026, it is genuinely unclear which entity "owns" region-wide coordination. LAHSA retains the CoC Collaborative Applicant role (and thus nominal responsibility for HUD compliance across the whole county) even as its operating budget and county staff have been stripped out and moved to HSH. This is not a hypothetical governance question — it is the live, unresolved subject of the August 6, 2026 federal court hearing. Reporters and advocates have explicitly flagged that no one has articulated what happens to the CoC application/compliance function if county capacity has moved elsewhere while the CoC "lead agency" designation has not. ([LAist coverage of the funding fight](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/los-angeles-homeless-funding-trump-permanent-housing-dollars-suspension-nofo-lawsuits-lahsa))
2. **DPSS vs. LAHSA/CES**: Family homeless assistance through CalWORKs (DPSS) is a parallel, means-tested system not obviously integrated with the CoC's Coordinated Entry System — a family that doesn't qualify for CalWORKs (e.g., certain immigration statuses, or single adults) has no equivalent DPSS pathway and must rely entirely on the CES/CoC system.
3. **Federal voucher supply (HACLA/LACDA) vs. local outreach/shelter capacity**: Programs like Inside Safe can move people out of encampments into interim housing, but if HACLA's voucher pipeline is paused for budget reasons, there is no guaranteed permanent-housing "exit," risking cycling back to homelessness or indefinite interim-housing stays — a structural gap between the entities that find people (city outreach) and the entities that permanently house them (federal-funded local housing authorities).
4. **City Council district-by-district variation**: Because each council office runs its own homelessness liaison operation, there is no single citywide standard for how/when encampment response, sanitation, and service-linkage happen — creating de facto policy variation within one city.
5. **DMH/DPH vs. CoC housing system**: Mental-health and public-health outreach teams (DMH's HOME, DPH's mobile clinics) operate their own caseloads and, per HSH's stated goals for FY2026-27, are only now being explicitly tasked with better coordinating with housing placement so that "housing and mental health efforts are pulling in the same direction instead of working in parallel" — an implicit admission that historically they have not been well coordinated. ([search summary re: HSH coordination goals](https://hoodline.com/2026/03/la-pols-greenlight-street-psych-blitz-to-tackle-homeless-crisis/))

**On the county audit/threat-to-leave saga**: The dispute did not resolve toward reconciliation — it resolved toward **institutional separation**. The county did not simply threaten to leave LAHSA; it followed through, building an entirely new department (HSH) and moving ~$300M+ and the bulk of direct-service capacity (via the Housing for Health absorption) into it. The city, in turn, voted to *study* leaving LAHSA too, though as of this writing it had not followed through, drawing pushback from Mayor Bass and several council members. What remains structurally unresolved is what LAHSA becomes: a smaller city-only homeless-services agency, a pure CoC/data-compliance shell, or something dissolved entirely. ([search summary, LAist "Will LA leave LAHSA?"](https://laist.com/brief/news/housing-homelessness/is-la-still-considering-making-a-break-from-the-regions-homelessness-agency))

## Tools & Systems

### HMIS (Homeless Management Information System)

HMIS is the HUD-mandated federal data infrastructure requirement: any CoC receiving HUD homeless-assistance funding must operate an HMIS, and any provider receiving CoC/ESG funds is generally required to enter client data into it. In LA, LAHSA administers HMIS on behalf of the CoC, using the vendor platform **Clarity Human Services** (built by **Bitfocus**). ([LAHSA HMIS page](https://www.lahsa.org/hmis/about); [Bitfocus HMIS product page](https://www.bitfocus.com/hmis-software))

**Known data-quality problems**: LAHSA commissioned its own Homeless Data Assessment (funded/summarized via the Conrad N. Hilton Foundation) that investigated the state of homeless data collection and management, based on interviews with LAHSA staff, its HMIS vendor, county departments, and other funders — an internal acknowledgment that data quality is a live concern rather than a solved problem. ([Hilton Foundation summary](https://www.hiltonfoundation.org/learning/los-angeles-homeless-data-assessment-report-issues-and-recommendations/))

More concretely and more damning: in 2026 reporting, LA city councilmembers reacted with fury (Councilmember Bob Blumenfield: "That's horrifying") on learning that **LAHSA's system allows providers to "bypass" logging when a person exits the Inside Safe motel-shelter program** — meaning the city could be paying for empty motel rooms for weeks after someone left, with no enforced requirement to log exits. The council has authorized $300 million for Inside Safe motel shelter under this data regime. ([LAist, "'Horrifying' Homeless Service Data Problems Prompt Fury From LA Councilmembers"](https://laist.com/news/housing-homelessness/inside-safe-homeless-service-data-problems-lahsa-la-councilmembers-bass))

**Who administers it**: LAHSA is LA's HMIS lead agency, using Clarity/Bitfocus as the software vendor. LAHSA also operates a client-facing "Portal" tied to Clarity that lets people experiencing homelessness view components of their own HMIS record and communicate with providers. ([LAHSA HMIS Portal announcement, summarized via search](https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=1027-hmis-client-portal))

### Coordinated Entry System (CES) and the assessment tool

LA's CES has historically used the **VI-SPDAT** (Vulnerability Index – Service Prioritization Decision Assistance Tool) to score and prioritize people for permanent supportive housing. As of 2025-2026, LA is in active transition to a successor tool, the **LA HAT (Los Angeles Housing Assessment Tool)**, which LAHSA describes as designed to fully replace the VI-SPDAT/CES Survey for Adults. Per LAHSA's own implementation documents, milestones were last updated January 2026 and FAQ materials updated April 2026, indicating the transition was still actively rolling out as of this research date. ([LAHSA LA HAT announcement](https://www.lahsa.org/news?article=1033-los-angeles-housing-assessment-tool-la-hat-); [LAHSA LA HAT implementation milestones PDF](https://www.lahsa.org/documents?id=9693-los-angeles-housing-assessment-tool-la-hat-implementation-milestones.pdf))

**Criticism of VI-SPDAT-style tools (documented, not merely anecdotal)**:
- **Racial and gender bias**: Peer-reviewed and advocacy research has found the VI-SPDAT systematically underperforms for Black populations, especially Black women — one study found white women scored consistently *higher* on vulnerability than Black women and most men, despite Black and white women reporting similar rates of trauma/abuse history, attributed to Black respondents' documented reluctance (rooted in historic mistrust of institutions) to disclose formal healthcare use, mental-health history, or illicit behavior to an intake worker — the very behaviors the tool scores on. ([HomelessHub summary of the research](https://homelesshub.ca/blog/2021/racial-and-gender-bias-vi-spdat/); [academic critique, ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/347388235_Invisible_intersectionality_in_measuring_vulnerability_among_individuals_experiencing_homelessness_-_critically_appraising_the_VI-SPDAT))
- **Weak validation**: The tool's creator organization, OrgCode Consulting, based the instrument heavily on data from a single northeastern U.S. city, from a largely male sample, analyzed for likelihood of *death* rather than validated against a "vulnerability" threshold — a methodological mismatch between what was measured and what the tool is used for. ([Shelterforce, "Common Homelessness Assessment Leads to Racial Disparities in Housing Placements"](https://shelterforce.org/2024/03/07/common-homelessness-assessment-leads-to-racial-disparities-in-housing-placements/))
- **Tool abandonment by its own creator**: Ian DeJong, the VI-SPDAT's creator, publicly disavowed it as an intake assessment tool years before OrgCode formally announced (December 2020) it would stop investing in the tool, explicitly citing "communities' persistent misuse of the tool" and the need to "accelerate activities to improve approaches that further promote racial and gender equity" — i.e., the tool's own authors concluded it was being gamed/misused and was itself inequitable. This is what triggered LA (and many other CoCs nationally) to begin developing successor tools like LA HAT. ([search summary of OrgCode's December 2020 announcement, via multiple secondary sources](https://legalclarity.org/vi-spdat-how-the-assessment-works-in-coordinated-entry/))
- **"Gaming" concern**: Because VI-SPDAT-type scores directly determine housing-resource priority, there has been documented concern (echoed in the broader literature on similar tools like Allegheny County's algorithmic housing-assessment tool) about both provider-side "coaching" of answers to boost scores and client-side underreporting due to stigma or distrust — both distort who actually gets prioritized. ([AAAI/ACM conference paper on a comparable tool, Allegheny Housing Assessment Tool](https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31636))

**LA HAT's design response**: Reporting indicates LA HAT is meant to separate "vulnerability" (harm a person faces if unhoused) from "acuity" (the level of services needed to stay housed), and to explicitly account for systemic barriers like housing discrimination and healthcare access rather than relying solely on individual-deficit questions — a direct response to the bias critiques above. **Gap flag**: this research did not find independent (non-LAHSA) evaluation of whether LA HAT actually reduces the documented racial disparities, since it is still mid-rollout as of 2026.

### Case management software: Clarity Human Services (Bitfocus)

Clarity is the cloud-based case-management platform LAHSA licenses from Bitfocus to serve as LA's HMIS front end; it's built to help providers stay compliant with HUD CoC/ESG and other federal-partner (PATH, VA, RHY, HOPWA) reporting requirements, with real-time reporting and a client portal. ([Bitfocus Clarity product page](https://www.bitfocus.com/coordinated-entry-products))

### Interoperability failures — the documented pain point

This is one of the more concretely documented failures in LA's system: **LA County has separately run a proprietary data system that does not talk to HMIS**, forcing some providers to manually double-enter client data into both systems. As one summary of the reporting put it, "manually double-entering things opens the door for all sorts of errors," and small numerical/data-entry errors can affect whether someone is recorded as sheltered or not — a direct, mechanical link between a data-architecture choice and a person's real housing status as recorded by the system. ([search summary citing California Health Care Foundation / Spectrum News reporting](https://spectrumnews1.com/ca/la/homelessness/2024/07/28/can-tech-help-solve-the-los-angeles-homeless-crisis--); [CHCF data exchange resource](https://www.chcf.org/resource/2025/01/01/homelessness-health-care/data-exchange/))

Separately, city and county entities have publicly acknowledged they are working to improve client-data sharing specifically between the homelessness and mental-health systems (i.e., HMIS/Clarity and DMH's own clinical records) — again, an admission that this integration has not historically existed. ([search summary](https://spectrumnews1.com/ca/la/homelessness/2024/07/28/can-tech-help-solve-the-los-angeles-homeless-crisis--))

The Inside Safe exit-logging "bypass" problem described above under HMIS is itself an interoperability/accountability failure: it means the mayor's signature program's outcome data is not reliably captured in the system meant to track it.

**Gap flag**: This research could not independently verify the *name or vendor* of LA County's proprietary non-HMIS data system referenced in secondary reporting — original county IT documentation was not accessible via the tools available for this research session. This should be verified against LA County ITA (Internal Services/IT) or DHS technical documentation if available.

### Encampment-tracking/resolution tools

- **LASAN's CARE/CARE+ program** (Comprehensive Cleaning and Rapid Engagement): Launched October 1, 2019 under then-Mayor Garcetti, run by LA Sanitation & Environment's Livability Services Division. At full deployment described in city records, it used 17 CARE teams and 13 CARE+ teams (CARE+ teams include LAHSA outreach workers embedded alongside sanitation staff) for regionally deployed encampment cleanups, targeting both routine trash/hazard removal and designated "special enforcement" zones (Skid Row, Venice Beach, etc.). The City Controller announced a performance audit of the program to assess its actual effectiveness against stated public-health goals. This research did not find a named, dedicated software/tracking platform specifically built for CARE/CARE+ deployment scheduling or outcome tracking (as distinct from HMIS/Clarity) — city documentation describes team structure and protocols (LAMC 56.11) but not a specific case-management or GIS tool by name. ([CARE Team Training materials, archive.org-hosted city document](https://archive.org/download/los-angeles-bureau-of-sanitation-training-materials-including-56.11-and-carecare/CARE%20Team%20Training.pdf); [LAMC 56.11 SOP](https://ia801001.us.archive.org/10/items/care-team-training/LASAN_LAMC_56.11_SOP_SIGNED_Amended_August_2018%20(4).pdf))
- **Inside Safe**: Operates via the Field Intervention Team model described above; this research found no evidence of a distinct, named encampment-tracking software system for Inside Safe separate from HMIS/Clarity and general city reporting to the Council/Controller — the "Homelessness Dashboard" published by City Controller Kenneth Mejia's office is the most visible public tracking tool and is explicitly a controller's-office accountability project, not an operational tool used by outreach teams themselves. ([Controller's Inside Safe dashboard](https://homelessdashboard.lacontroller.app/InsideSafe))
- **County encampment response**: LA County Public Health issues countywide **Encampment Resolution Guidance** for local jurisdictions (a policy document, not a software tool), and the new HSH department is described as "overseeing encampment outreach efforts" — but this research did not identify a named county encampment-tracking software system distinct from HMIS. ([LA County Encampment Resolution Guidance PDF](https://file.lacounty.gov/SDSInter/lac/1183858_LACountyEncampmentResolutionGuidance.pdf))

**Gap flag**: Despite the volume of city/county reporting on encampment operations, this research did not surface a named, purpose-built encampment-tracking IT system (comparable to how Clarity/HMIS is named for case management) — encampment tracking appears to happen through some combination of Clarity/HMIS entries, LASAN's own internal systems, council-office spreadsheets, and after-the-fact Controller's-office dashboards, rather than one authoritative real-time system. If such a system exists under a specific name, it was not found via the tools available in this research session and should be checked directly with LASAN or HSH.

### Research-access notes (methodology transparency)

During this research session, the WebFetch tool returned HTTP 403 errors for the large majority of direct page-fetch attempts (including laist.com, lahsa.org, dhs.lacounty.gov, homeless.lacounty.gov, lacounty.gov, cbsnews.com, hud.gov, and even neutral test domains like example.com and wikipedia.org), suggesting an environment-level network/proxy restriction rather than a per-site block. As a result, **most claims above are sourced from WebSearch result summaries** (which themselves cite and quote the underlying articles) rather than from directly fetched and independently re-read full-text pages. Direct URLs are provided throughout so a human researcher (or a future session with working fetch access) can verify each claim against the primary page text. Any figure marked with a "gap flag" above should be treated as lower-confidence and re-verified before publication.
